“Digital Image-Guided vs. Manual Marking Approaches for Toric IOL Implantation: A Summary of Clinical Evidence”

Updated Sept 8, 2026  ·  7 min read

Summary

Accurate rotational alignment of toric intraocular lenses (IOLs) is one of the strongest predictors of visual outcome in astigmatism-correcting cataract surgery. Small errors compound quickly: published estimates put the refractive penalty at roughly a few percent of residual astigmatism for every degree the lens drifts from its intended meridian.

Manual marking — ink, mechanical, or slit-lamp based — has long been the default approach, but it is sensitive to operator technique, cyclotorsion between the seated and supine position, fixation shifts, and marking-ink spread. Digital image-guided systems attempt to remove much of that variability by fusing preoperative biometry with an intraoperative, landmark-based overlay that tracks the eye in real time.

This backgrounder reviews a set of clinical studies and a pooled meta-analysis comparing the two approaches. Across the reviewed evidence, digital guidance is associated with tighter alignment tolerances and lower mean residual astigmatism than manual marking — though the size of the benefit varies by study, and it does not always translate into a visual acuity difference patients would notice.
